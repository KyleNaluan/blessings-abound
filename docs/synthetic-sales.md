# Synthetic sales history

This page is generated by `uv run python -m app.synthetic_calibration` in `api/`, so edit the script or `SyntheticSales` in `api/app/settings.py` instead of this page.

**Every number here, and every chart drawn from this history, is synthetic.**
The store has no sales history to show, so the dashboards run on one made by a model calibrated to published figures.
Any seasonal pattern in the charts was put there by the seasonal index below, so the dashboards show what the app can do, not a finding about the store.

The hold-out comes out at 19.9% against 28.0%, 29.0% low, which is outside the 25% band.
No parameter was changed to move it, because a hold-out that is tuned to stops being a check.

### Shape of the time to sell

A study of Mercari Japan reports a mean time to sell of 19.4 days with a standard deviation of 50 and a maximum of more than 1,400 days, which is many quick sales and a long tail.
The figure is used only to check the shape, so the check passes when the mean is above the median, the skewness is above 0, and the slowest tenth of sales take at least 3 times the median.
It is judged on Channel A, because Mercari is a general marketplace like eBay, and Channel B's rising curve puts its sales late in the run by design.

Channel A passes the shape check.
No sale can take longer than the 90-day run, so the tail stops far short of Mercari's 1,400 days.

## How the history is made

Each month a number of items is forwarded to Ecommerce, drawn around an average of `monthly_items`, on days spread through the month.
Each item comes from the clothing or the electronics and games department, and gets the category, apparel department and condition the app already uses, with the shares below.
Its typical price is drawn from a log-normal spread around each category's middle price.
The appraisal says "check" for a share of items, and otherwise yes at or above the $30 worth-listing bar in app/settings.py and no below it.
Staff accept or reject each item at a rate for its department and recommendation, and a call against a yes or a no counts as an override.
An accepted item is listed a few days later on one channel, Channel A for games and hardware and either channel for apparel, at its typical price.

Each listing then runs day by day for up to 90 days.
Its chance of selling on a day is 1 - exp(-r), where r is the channel's baseline for that day of the run multiplied by the factors for the item's price band, category and condition and the seasonal index for that day's month.
The baseline is the rise over that day in a Weibull curve's cumulative rate, (d / scale) ** shape, so with every factor at 1 the share sold within d days is 1 - exp(-(d / scale) ** shape).
Channel A's eBay baseline has a shape below 1, so its daily chance falls as a listing ages, with most sales early and a few much later.
Channel B's Poshmark baseline has a shape above 1, so its daily chance rises as a listing ages.
A listing that hasn't sold by the end of its run ends on its 90th day.

One random number stream from the seed drives every draw in a fixed order, so the same seed always gives the same rows, and `uv run python -m app.synthetic_sales` replaces both tables with them in one transaction.
The history lives in `synthetic_items` and `synthetic_listings`, apart from the real `items`, `sale_events` and `sync_log`, as [database.md](database.md#synthetic-sales-history) describes.

## Sources

- FlowLister's [eBay listing data study](https://flowlister.com/ebay-listing-data-study/) of 33,638 single-unit eBay US listings: 12.2% sold within 30 days, 5.8% within a week, a median of 7.5 days to sale among those sold within 30 days, and 30-day sell-through of 8.6% under $15 against 27.6% at $100 to $249.99.
- Closo's [comparison of clothing marketplaces](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/top-clothing-marketplaces-for-resale): women's tops on Poshmark averaged 59 days to sale with 37% sell-through, and tops on eBay had 28% sell-through over 90 days, which is the hold-out.
- A study of Mercari Japan with a mean time to sell of 19.4 days, a standard deviation of 50 and a maximum of more than 1,400 days, used only for the shape check.
- A thrift retail practitioner's article saying seasonal clothing sales peak at the start of the season and fall off after, which is the only support for the seasonal index, so its values are assumed.
- Nothing published backs the intake and funnel rates, which are the share forwarded, accepted, rejected and overridden in each department, so they are assumed.

## Limits

- FlowLister and Closo sell tools to resellers, so their figures are samples they report about their own users, not audited data.
- The app has no category for tops, so the Closo figures are compared with women's apparel on each channel.
- Closo's Poshmark figures cover a trailing year rather than a 90-day run, so matching a 59-day average inside 90 days needs Channel B's chance to rise with age.
  That rise is a fit to the figure, not evidence about how Poshmark sells.
- Each item is listed on one channel, while the app lists an item on every channel that takes it, because the published figures are for one marketplace each and a sale on one channel would cut the other's sell-through.
- Groups with few listings, such as items under $15, move a lot from one seed to the next, as the spread shows, so one history can be far from the published figure even when the mean is close.
- The sale price is the asking price, because the history has no offers or price drops.
